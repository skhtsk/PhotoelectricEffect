The 4 Experimental Results: Why Classical Wave Theory Failed

In 1905, Albert Einstein resolved the crisis of the photoelectric effect by adopting Max Planck’s quantum hypothesis: light is not a continuous wave front, but localized discrete packets of energy called photons, each with energy $E = hf$.

Result 1 f < f₀ → No Emission

Existence of a Threshold Frequency ($f_0$)

No electrons are emitted unless the incident light frequency exceeds a cutoff value $f_0$, characteristic of the target metal, no matter how bright the light is.

❌ Classical Wave Theory Prediction: Light wave carries continuous energy proportional to intensity ($I \propto A^2$). Any frequency should eventually eject electrons if the beam is made bright enough.
✓ Photon (Particle) Theory Explanation: Light consists of discrete quanta ($E = hf$). Emission is a 1-to-1 photon-electron interaction. If $hf < \Phi$, no single photon delivers sufficient work to liberate an electron.
Result 2 Δt < 10⁻⁹ s

Instantaneous Emission (No Time Delay)

Electrons are emitted almost instantly (time lag $< 10^{-9}\text{ s}$) as soon as light of $f \ge f_0$ strikes the metal, even under extremely weak / dim illumination.

❌ Classical Wave Theory Prediction: Wave energy spreads evenly across trillions of surface atoms. For dim light, an electron would need hours, days, or weeks to accumulate enough energy ($\approx 2\text{--}5\text{ eV}$) to escape!
✓ Photon (Particle) Theory Explanation: Energy is concentrated into a localized packet. When a photon collides with an electron, its entire energy $hf$ is transferred in a single instant. If $hf \ge \Phi$, emission is immediate.
Result 3 KE_max = hf - Φ

Frequency Increases Maximum Kinetic Energy

The maximum kinetic energy $KE_{\max}$ (and stopping potential $V_s$) depends linearly on light frequency $f$, and is completely independent of light intensity!

❌ Classical Wave Theory Prediction: Higher intensity light has larger electric field amplitude ($E_0$), exerting a greater oscillating force on electrons and giving them higher kinetic energy. Frequency should not dictate energy.
✓ Photon (Particle) Theory Explanation: By conservation of energy: $hf = \Phi + KE_{\max} \implies KE_{\max} = hf - \Phi$. Increasing frequency directly boosts the photon's energy packet, leaving more surplus energy as kinetic energy.
Result 4 Emission Rate & Intensity

Emission Rate vs. Intensity and Frequency

At a fixed frequency $f > f_0$, emission rate is directly proportional to intensity. However, at constant intensity, increasing frequency reduces the emission rate!

⚡ Constant Frequency ($f = \text{const}$): Doubling intensity doubles beam energy, which doubles the number of photons arriving per second ($N/t$). Hence, twice as many electrons are liberated per second (photocurrent doubles).
🔍 Constant Intensity ($I = \text{const}$): Intensity is beam power: $I = (N/t) \times hf \implies N/t = \frac{I}{hf}$. As frequency $f$ increases, each photon carries more energy, meaning fewer photons per second strike the surface! Thus, at constant beam intensity, the emission rate decreases as frequency increases (for $f > f_0$).

Direct Comparison Summary

Feature Wave Theory Expectation Experimental Fact (Particle Model)
Threshold Frequency ($f_0$) None; any light can cause emission if bright enough. Strict $f_0 = \Phi / h$. No emission if $f < f_0$ at any intensity.
Emission Time Delay Measurable time lag (minutes/hours) for dim light to accumulate energy. Instantaneous ($< 10^{-9}\text{ s}$), as single photons deliver energy all at once.
Maximum Kinetic Energy ($KE_{\max}$) Increases with light intensity (larger wave amplitude). Increases linearly with frequency ($hf - \Phi$), independent of intensity.
Role of Light Intensity Determines the energy transferred to individual electrons. Determines photon arrival rate; increases photocurrent, NOT individual $KE_{\max}$.
